Quarterly review

Q3 platform reliability review

Three quarters into the reliability programme, availability is ahead of target and incident volume is down by half — but recovery time has stopped improving, and that is now the binding constraint on the enterprise tier.

Date 30 September 2026
Author Dana Whitfield
Team Platform Engineering
Version v1.2 · Final

Executive summary

The platform met its 99.9% availability commitment in every month of Q3, closing the quarter at 99.96%. Severity-1 incidents fell from twelve in Q2 to five, and no incident this quarter was caused by a change that had passed the new pre-deploy gate.

Mean time to recovery, however, was flat at 41 minutes against a 25-minute target. The cause is diagnosis, not repair: two thirds of recovery time is spent identifying which service is at fault. We recommend funding distributed tracing in Q4 and deferring the multi-region read replicas, which address a failure mode we have not seen in a year.

01

Where the quarter landed

Availability improved for the third consecutive quarter and is now comfortably above the contractual floor. The improvement is not evenly distributed: the API and publishing paths carried the gains, while the ingest pipeline accounted for four of the five severity-1 incidents and all of the customer-visible data delay.

99.96% Availability, against a 99.90% commitment
5 Severity-1 incidents, down from 12 in Q2
41 min Mean time to recovery, unchanged, target 25 min

The pre-deploy gate introduced in July is the single clearest win. It has blocked thirty-one changes, of which nine were later confirmed to carry defects that would have reached production. No incident this quarter originated in a change that passed it.

02

Why recovery time stopped improving

We instrumented every severity-1 timeline this quarter and split it into detection, diagnosis and repair. The shape is consistent enough to act on.

  • Detection is solved. Median time from fault to page is 90 seconds, down from 6 minutes a year ago. Alert quality work is finished.
  • Diagnosis is the bottleneck. A median of 27 of the 41 minutes is spent deciding which service is at fault. Responders reconstruct request paths by reading logs in four systems that do not share an identifier.
  • Repair is fast when the target is known. Once a service is named, median repair is 9 minutes, mostly a rollback or a scale-up.
  • The pattern is worst across team boundaries. Incidents spanning two or more owning teams took 2.4× longer to diagnose than single-team incidents.
“We are not slow at fixing things. We are slow at agreeing on what is broken.” — Postmortem, INC-2291, 19 August
03

The Q4 trade-off

Two proposals compete for the same two engineers. Multi-region read replicas were committed in the annual plan; distributed tracing was not. On the evidence of this quarter, the priority should be reversed.

Distributed tracing — recommended

A single request identifier propagated across the four systems responders already use. Estimated at six engineer-weeks with no new vendor. If it removes even half of the diagnosis time, MTTR lands at 27 minutes, within touching distance of target.

Multi-region read replicas — defer

This addresses a regional outage, a failure mode with no occurrence in the last four quarters and no customer commitment attached. It is roughly fourteen engineer-weeks and adds a standing operational cost of about $6,400 a month. Nothing about it is wrong; it is simply not what is hurting us.
