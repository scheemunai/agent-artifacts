01

Postgres 18 and the end of the connection pooler

Hexadecimal 24 min Published 06:40

TL;DR

Built-in connection multiplexing lands in 18, and for fleets under roughly 500 clients it removes the case for running PgBouncer at all. Above that, the pooler still wins on failover behaviour.

Key points

  • Benchmarks: 12k connections held at 41 MB of backend memory, down from 1.7 GB on 17 with the same workload.
  • Caveat: prepared statements still pin a backend, so the win shrinks for ORMs that prepare aggressively.
  • Migration: the presenter kept the pooler in front for a week and cut over by lowering its pool size to zero — no client changes.

Worth watching if you own the database tier.

02

We replaced our vector DB with a single table

Latent Space 38 min Published 09:05

TL;DR

A 40-million-chunk corpus moved from a managed vector store into pgvector with an HNSW index. Recall held at 0.94, p95 latency rose 11 ms, and the bill fell by $9.4k a month.

Key points

  • The unlock was hybrid search: full-text and vector scores are merged in one query, which the managed service billed as two.
  • Index build is the pain: nine hours on a 16-core box, done offline against a replica and promoted.
  • Don't do this below ~2 million chunks — the operational cost outweighs the savings.

Worth watching if your retrieval bill has a comma in it.

03

The cost curve nobody budgets for

Infra Weekly 17 min Published 11:20

TL;DR

Egress, not compute, is what turns a cheap architecture expensive. A worked example shows a $300/month cluster carrying $4,100/month of cross-zone traffic because of one misplaced replica.

Key points

  • Rule of thumb: price every hop that crosses an availability zone before you price the instances.
  • Topology-aware routing fixed 80% of it in one config change.
  • Watch for: observability agents shipping full payloads — the second-largest line item in the example.

Worth watching if you sign off the cloud invoice.

04

Shipping agents that other teams actually adopt

Build Notes 52 min Published 14:02

TL;DR

Adoption tracked with one thing: whether the agent produced an artifact a human could forward. Teams whose agents only answered in chat churned within two weeks.

Key points

  • Durable output beats chat: a link that survives the session is what gets shared into other teams.
  • Name the owner: agents with a named human sponsor were three times more likely to still run after 90 days.
  • Keep the surface small: the most-adopted internal agent had four tools, not forty.

Worth watching if you are rolling agents out internally.

05

A field guide to flaky end-to-end tests

The Green Build 40 min Published 16:35

TL;DR

Four flake families cover almost everything: unawaited state, shared fixtures, clock drift and resource contention. Each gets a detection recipe you can run against existing CI logs.

Key points

  • Quarantine, don't retry: blanket retries hid a real race for seven months in the case study.
  • Measure flake rate per test, not per suite — one test was 60% of all failures.
  • Freeze the clock in fixtures; two of the four families disappear.

Worth watching if your CI is red more than it's green.
